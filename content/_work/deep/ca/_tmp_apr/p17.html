Telangana Becomes First Indian State to Implement SC Sub-CategorisationIn April 2025, the Government of Telangana notified the implementation of the ‘Telangana Scheduled Castes (Rationalisation of Reservations) Act, 2025’, through a gazette notification, marking it as the first state in India to implement sub-categorization of Scheduled Castes (SCs), also known as reservation within reservation, by classifying 59 SC sub-castes into three groups for reservations in education and employment.

  • The Telangana Government based its decision on empirical data, including the social, economic, educational, employment, and political status of SC communities.

Background:
i.On August 1, 2024, a 7-judge Supreme Court of India (SCI) bench upheld the constitutional validity of sub-classifying SCs and Scheduled Tribes (STs) to ensure equitable reservation benefits.
ii.On March 18, 2025, the Telangana Legislative Assembly passed the SC sub-categorization Bill, aligning with the apex court’s verdict.
iii.The Telangana Scheduled Castes (Rationalisation of Reservation) Act, 2025 received the Governor’s assent on April 8, 2025, and was subsequently published in the Telangana Gazette on April 14, 2025.
Three-Tier Categorisation:
The SCs have been divided into three groups from a total of 59 sub-castes:
1.Group-I (Most Backward Sub-Castes):

  • 15 sub-castes (0.5% of Telangana’s SC population) are classified as “most backward.”
  • Granted 1% reservation (double their population share) to enhance access to education and employment.

2.Group-II (Marginally Benefited Sub-Castes):

  • 18 sub-castes receive marginal benefits.
  • Allocated 9% reservation.

3.Group-III (Relatively Better Placed Sub-Castes):

  • 26 sub-castes with better access to opportunities.
  • Granted 5% reservation.

Note: Out of the total 59 sub-castes, 33 sub-castes continue in their earlier groups. Only 26 sub-castes, constituting 3.43% of the SC population, have been reclassified under the new categorisation.

Governor – Jishnu Dev Varma
Capital – Hyderabad

UAE Elected Chair of INTERPOL Committee on GovernanceIn April 2025, the Ministry of Interior of the United Arab Emirates (UAE) has been elected to chair International Criminal Police Organization (INTERPOL)’s Committee on Governance, one of the key advisory bodies of INTERPOL. With this, UAE became the 1st country to chair this newly formed standing committee, and will serve 2 year-term.

  • Lieutenant Colonel Dana Humaid Al Marzouqi, Director General (DG) of the International Affairs Bureau at the Ministry of Interior (UAE) got 67% of the total votes from the representatives of INTERPOL member states during the committee’s session held at Lyon, France.
  • As part of the committee’s mandate, UAE will be responsible for steering discussions on INTERPOL’s governance, representing the committee in reports to the General Assembly and the Executive Committee and ensuring co-ordination among member states to enhance the functioning of INTERPOL.

About INTERPOL’s Committee on Governance:
i.The committee was established in November 2024 by a resolution during the 92nd General Assembly of INTERPOL held in Glasgow, the United Kingdom (UK).
ii.The committee is entrusted with the responsibility to monitor the review of INTERPOL’s statutes, regulations, and related documents, proposing necessary amendments or clarifications.
iii.It comprises of high-level experts in governance and international law from member states which ensures wider, inclusive participation of many member states in the committee’s work.
iv.It will also provide assistance to the General Assembly on how to interpret and enhance the foundational legal frameworks, thus ensuring transparency and efficiency in its operations.

NPCI Revises UPI Framework with New Transaction LimitsIn April 2025, the Mumbai (Maharashtra) based National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI) introduced significant revisions to the Unified Payments Interface (UPI) framework, focusing on enhancing security and curbing fraud risks effective from 4th April 2025.

  • The major changes include the discontinuation of international Quick Response (QR) code payments, limits on domestic QR code transactions with unverified merchants, and stricter wallet reload protocols.

Key Revisions:
International QR Code Payments Discontinued:

  • NPCI has banned QR-based international UPI transactions, specifically the “QR Share & Pay” method, effective from April 4, 2025.
  • Indian users abroad, including Non-Resident Indians (NRIs), will no longer be able to scan QR codes shared via messaging apps like WhatsApp or other social media platforms for making payments.
  • Live, in-person scanning at merchant locations overseas will still be possible, but the convenience of scanning saved QR codes will no longer be allowed.

Domestic Transaction Cap on QR Payments:

  • A limit of Rs.2,000 per transaction has been set for QR Share & Pay transactions made to non-verified offline merchants within India.
  • This measure aims to limit fraud in peer-to-merchant (P2M) transactions and encourages the formal registration of small merchants.

Prohibition on Collect Requests for Wallet Reloading:

  • NPCI has prohibited merchants from initiating collect requests to load prepaid wallets or gift cards, which had been prone to exploitation in scams.
  • Users will now need to initiate wallet reloads themselves, using “push requests” to enhance control and reduce unauthorised deductions.

About National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI):
Managing Director (MD) and Chief Executive Officer (CEO) – Dilip Asbe
Headquarters – Mumbai, Maharashtra
Established – 2008

RBI Imposes Rs. 3.20 Lakh Penalty on Citibank for FEMA Violations
In April 2025, Mumbai (Maharashtra) based Reserve Bank of India (RBI) imposed a monetary penalty of Rs. 3,20,000 on New York (United State of America,USA) based Citibank N.A. for contravention of  Section 10 (4) of FEMA, 1999, which mandates adherence to the regulations governing foreign exchange transactions.

  • The penalty was levied under Section 11(3) of FEMA for the bank’s failure in conducting proper due diligence while processing inward remittances.
  • The action was taken because Citibank N.A. failed to undertake the necessary due diligence while processing inward remittances from a Foreign Currency Account opened by one of its constituents.

i.TheRBI had issued a Show Cause Notice to Citibank N.A., seeking an explanation for the alleged violation. The bank responded with a written reply and also presented oral submissions.
ii.After careful consideration of the facts and the bank’s response, the RBI concluded that the identified violations were substantiated and warranted the imposition of a monetary penalty.

Cricket Returns to Olympics After 128 Years in LA Olympics 2028
In April 2025, Lausanne, Switzerland based International Olympic Committee (IOC) confirmed that cricket will make its return to the Olympic Games at the 2028 Los Angeles Olympics, after a gap of 128 years.

  • The sport will feature six teams each in men’s and women’s T20 (Twenty20) tournaments, marking its first Olympic appearance since the 1900 Paris Games, where a one-off match between Great Britain and France was held.

Event Overview
i.Format: Matches will follow the T20 format, with six teams per gender competing for medals.
ii.Athlete Quota: A total of 90 athlete quotas are allocated for each gender, allowing 15-member squads per team.

  • The IOC also approved a record 351 medal events for LA 2028, including 22 additional events compared to Paris 2024.
  • The total athlete quota remains at 10,500, with an extra 698 spots allocated to the new sports.

iii.Qualification: The qualification criteria are yet to be confirmed. The United States of America (USA) is likely to secure a direct spot as the host nation. Other teams will qualify based on the International Cricket Council (ICC) rankings.
iv.Venues: Specific locations for cricket matches in Los Angeles (USA) remain unconfirmed, with schedules to be finalized closer to the event.
v.ICC Membership: The ICC comprises 12 full-member nations including India, Australia, England and 94 associate members.

World Voice Day 2025 – April 16World Voice Day (WVD) is an annual global event observed on April 16 to celebrate the significance of the human voice in communication, identity, and culture.

  • It is a day dedicated to recognising the significance of the human voice, highlighting its vital role in everyday communication, professional interaction, and artistic expression.

Theme:
i.The 2025 campaign, themed “Empower Your Voice,” emphasizes vocal health awareness and the importance of nurturing one’s voice.
ii.The theme was chosen by the Voice Committee of the American Academy of Otolaryngology–Head and Neck Surgery (AAO-HNS).
Background:
i.WVD originated in Brazil on April 16, 1999, initiated by the Brazilian Society of Laryngology and Voice (SBLV) under the leadership of Dr. Nedio Steffen. The event began as Brazilian Voice Day, aiming to raise awareness about voice health and disorders.
ii.The Brazilian initiative inspired countries like Argentina and Portugal to adopt similar observances.
iii.In 2002, the AAO-HNS officially recognized the day, renaming it World Voice Day(WVD) and promoting it internationally
Significance:
The day serves as a global platform to:

MP Declares 25th WLS Named After Dr B.R. Ambedkar in Sagar DistrictIn April 2025, the Government of Madhya Pradesh (MP) declared its 25th wildlife sanctuary (WLS), named Dr Bhimrao Ambedkar Abhyaran, covering an area of 258.64 square kilometres (sq. km) in Sagar district, Madhya Pradesh (MP).

  • The WLS was notified under the Wildlife Protection Act, 1972, and was inaugurated on 14th April 2025, coinciding with the 134th birth anniversary of Dr. Bhimrao Ramji Ambedkar.
  • It has been designated to strengthen wildlife conservation, preserve biodiversity, and support sustainable eco-tourism and employment in MP, which is widely recognized as the “Tiger State of India”.

About Dr Bhimrao Ambedkar Abhyaran:
i.Dr Bhimrao Ambedkar Abhyaran is located within the North Sagar Forest Division, covering forested areas in Banda Tehsil and Shahgarh region of Sagar district.
ii.It will act as a protected corridor between Panna Tiger Reserve (Bundelkhand region) and Madhav Tiger Reserve (Gwalior-Chambal region) of MP.

  • The corridor will contribute towards the formation of the longest and largest tiger corridor in North-Central India, enhancing the movement of tigers, cheetahs, leopards, wolves, gharials, and vultures.

iii.It enhances connectivity between Madhav Tiger Reserve and Ranthambore Tiger Reserve in Sawai Madhopur (Rajasthan), passing through Kuno National Park in Sheopur (MP), which is home to African cheetahs.
iv.Some notable WLS in MP are Pench, Kanha, Bandhavgarh, Satpura, and Panna Tiger Reserves.
Key Points:
i.Ranthambore National Park, located in Sawai Madhopur, Rajasthan, is one of the largest and most renowned tiger reserves in India.
ii.Among all states and Union Territories (UTs), the Andaman and Nicobar (A&N) Islands have the highest number of WLS, with a total of 97.
